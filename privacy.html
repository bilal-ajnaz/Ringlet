Privacy policy

Updated 15 November 2026

Ringlet works on your device and collects nothing. Your sounds never leave your devices, except to your own iCloud if you turn on iCloud sync.

What Ringlet uses, and why

  • Songs stored on your device: listed so you can cut a ring from one you own. Ringlet asks before it reads your music library. On Mac, it lists the songs in your Music folder.
  • The microphone: used only while you record your voice for a ring. Ringlet asks first.
  • Files, videos and photos you pick: opened only when you choose them, to take a sound or a label photo.
  • Alarms: set through iOS so your ring can wake you. Ringlet asks for alarm permission the first time.

What stays where

Your rings, records and settings are stored on your device. If you use the Apple Watch app or widgets, Ringlet passes them between your own devices through iOS. There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking, and Ringlet has no servers.

iCloud sync

With Ringlet Pro you can sync your rings between your iPhone, iPad and Mac. When iCloud sync is on, your rings, the sounds they were cut from and their label photos are stored in your own private iCloud database, using Apple's CloudKit. Only your Apple Account can read them; the developer can't see them. Sample rings and alarms stay on each device. Turn it off any time in Ringlet's Settings, and manage or delete the data in your device's iCloud settings.

Purchases

Ringlet Pro is bought through the App Store. Apple handles the payment; Ringlet only learns whether Pro is unlocked.

Children

Ringlet collects no data from anyone, including children.
